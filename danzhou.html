When I arrived I knew nothing about Danzhou. A year on, some of what matters is not in any textbook: the weather, the food, and the way people talk.

Weather: get used to this first

  • Rain: it arrives fast and falls hard. Keep an umbrella in your bag and do not judge by the sky.
  • Humidity: clothes and books go damp. Put dehumidifier packs in your wardrobe.
  • Typhoon season: follow the forecast and the university notices. Keep water and food in, and do not go out in it.
  • Sun: the midday sun is harsher than you expect. Sunscreen is not just for one half of the class.

Talking to people

  • The local dialect is not Mandarin, and it sounds nothing like it. Not understanding it and being unwelcome are two different things.
  • Haggling at markets and small shops works differently here. Watch how other people buy first.
  • For older shopkeepers, "ayi" (auntie) or "ashu" (uncle) is more usual than "boss".

A few honest notes

  • Go out in company at night, and share your trip with your roommates when you take a taxi.
  • Feeling out of place at the start is normal. The first month is the hardest part.
  • Ask your tutor or an older student when something is wrong. You do not have to work it out alone.

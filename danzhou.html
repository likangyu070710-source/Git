Once you leave the gate

Living in Danzhou

Two things I did not expect. The first is the language: the local dialect is not Mandarin, and I cannot follow a word of it. The second is that the typhoons, which sound apocalyptic in the forecast, mostly pass over Danzhou without the force you brace for.

Weather: get used to this first

  • Sun: the one thing I genuinely cannot stand. It is strong enough to change your skin colour in an afternoon, and it does not feel dangerous while it is happening.
  • Typhoons: there is usually one a year, but in Danzhou itself I have never felt the force the forecast sounds like. Read the notices anyway.
  • Humidity: clothes and paper go damp. Dehumidifier packs in the wardrobe are worth the few yuan.

Talking to people

  • The local dialect is not Mandarin, and I cannot follow any of it.
  • Older shopkeepers are where it bites. Expect to point at what you want, or to have the number written down for you.

Weekends

Town for the afternoon

Enough for the big shopping and a film. One afternoon covers it.

The coast or a nearby town

Worth the trip when the weather is good — but check the last bus back.

Staying on campus

Honestly, most of my weekends were the library and the dorm. That is not a failure.

A few honest notes

  • Go out in company at night, and share your trip with your roommates when you take a taxi.
  • Feeling out of place at the start is normal. The first month is the hardest part.
  • Ask your tutor or an older student when something is wrong. You do not have to work it out alone.
